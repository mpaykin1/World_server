The current construction uses:

- a separate `CircleGeometry` crater;

- three strong semantic slope channels plus two secondary strokes;
- object-specific framing and watercolor normalization.

## Why this body still reads wrong

Engineering diagnosis from the rejected implementation:

1. **The silhouette is still generated like a parametric 3D object.** The reference reads as one hand-painted mountain mass, not as a mathematically regular radial surface.
2. **The crater is assembled from separate circle/torus primitives.** Even after deformation, this can read as a constructed 3D ring rather than an irregular painted opening.
3. **The dark channels are explicit 3D tube strokes.** Their spacing and continuity can feel designed/technical instead of naturally wandering painted gullies.
4. **The shape hierarchy is wrong when the geometry is noticed before the wash.** In the reference, the broad watercolor mass comes first; only then do a few dark lines define the slopes.
5. **A numeric image-space score can be gamed by matching coverage, luminance and edge density while still missing the gestalt.** The gate is useful for regression, but it cannot replace human artistic judgment.

## Next implementation rule

The next volcano must be rebuilt as a **painted mass first**, not as a cone with decorative lines.

Preferred direction:

- generate an asymmetric silhouette from a 2D/2.5D reference profile;
- use fewer, broader overlapping watercolor masses to define the mountain;
- make the crater an irregular negative/dark painted shape, not a technical ring;
- derive slope strokes from the silhouette and paint flow rather than fixed tubes;
- keep the successful smoke unchanged;
- require human side-by-side acceptance even if the automated gate is >=85.

## Do not repeat

Do not claim volcano success solely because:
- reference coverage is close;
- luminance/edge metrics pass;
- the automated score exceeds 85;
- the shape is technically 3D and animated.

For this object, **human visual acceptance is the final gate**.
